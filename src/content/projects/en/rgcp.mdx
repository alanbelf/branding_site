## The situation

A university research computing group needed GPU compute after its
on-premises systems were taken offline. Researchers needed to keep working
while replacement hardware was procured and built, a process that could
take months.

## Constraints

- There was no dedicated operations team; one person had to build and run the platform alongside other full-time responsibilities.
- The platform was temporary and needed to be easy to wind down when the new hardware went live.
- Researchers needed clear usage limits and cost visibility to manage cloud GPU spending.
- Onboarding had to support both GPU beginners and experienced researchers.

## Approach

I built a self-service GPU cloud platform on HyperStack so researchers
could provision compute without filing tickets. The platform included
usage visibility and limits to help control spending. I wrote onboarding
materials for beginner and advanced users and ran live sessions.

## Key decisions

Self-service provisioning let researchers request compute without waiting
for a one-person ticket queue. It also required usage limits to manage
spending.

I included cost visibility and limits in the platform rather than relying
on reports after the spending had occurred.

I kept the platform scoped as a temporary bridge so it could be
decommissioned when the replacement on-premises system was ready.

## Outcome

Researchers continued working during the hardware transition. The
platform is now being wound down as the replacement on-premises system
approaches go-live.
