## The situation

A university research computing group needed continued access to GPU
compute after its on-prem systems were taken offline. Researchers
couldn't pause their work while a new on-prem system was procured and
built out — a compute gap that could stretch for months had to be
bridged with something usable almost immediately.

## Constraints

- No dedicated ops team — this had to be built and run by one person alongside other full-time responsibilities
- Temporary by design — the platform needed to be easy to wind down once new hardware went live, not a permanent fixture
- Cost had to stay predictable and visible — researchers are not infrastructure specialists and can easily overspend on cloud GPU time without guardrails
- Users ranged from GPU beginners to advanced researchers, so onboarding had to work for both

## Approach

I designed and stood up a self-service GPU cloud platform on
HyperStack, giving researchers a way to provision compute themselves
rather than filing tickets and waiting. On top of that, I built cost
governance into the platform from day one — usage visibility and
limits — so that self-service didn't turn into runaway spend. I also
produced onboarding material covering both beginner and advanced
usage scenarios, and ran live onboarding sessions to get users
comfortable quickly.

## Key decisions

**Self-service provisioning instead of a ticket-based request queue.**
A ticket queue doesn't scale with one person behind it, and it adds
friction exactly when researchers need to move fast. Self-service
removed me as a bottleneck, at the cost of needing stronger guardrails
around spend.

**Cost governance built into the platform, not bolted on as reporting.**
After-the-fact usage reports catch overspend too late to prevent it.
Building limits and visibility into the platform itself meant problems
were caught before they became a budget conversation.

**Treated as explicitly temporary, not over-built.**
Over-engineering a bridge solution wastes effort that should go toward
the permanent on-prem system. Scoping it as temporary kept the build
lean and easy to decommission cleanly.

## Outcome

Researchers kept working through the hardware transition without a
compute gap. The platform is now being wound down in a controlled way
as the new on-prem system approaches go-live, exactly as designed from
the start.
