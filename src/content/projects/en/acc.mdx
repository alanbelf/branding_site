## The situation

A university research group needed CPU-based HPC capacity while its
long-term on-premises environment was unavailable. The gap was expected to
last six months, after which cloud capacity would serve as burst capacity.
Ten to twenty researchers ran tightly coupled MPI applications for
molecular dynamics, computational chemistry, and atmospheric and ocean
modeling, as well as high-memory genomics and data science workloads. They
needed shared scientific software, secure institutional access, and
project budget controls.

## Constraints

- A small administrative team would run the platform, so it needed managed orchestration and automation.
- The planned six-month lifecycle favored pay-as-you-go capacity that could scale to zero over fixed reservations.
- Multi-node MPI and memory-heavy single-node jobs had to share one scheduling policy.
- Access had to stay on a private network and use institutional identity and multi-factor authentication.
- A fixed project budget required active capacity planning.

## Approach

I designed the complete cluster architecture, Slurm scheduling policies,
storage, identity model, budget controls, and scientific software stack. 
Because the environment resided under the university's Azure tenant, their
infrastructure team provisioned the base Azure resources from 
my specifications. Once deployed, they handed over full operational control of
the Azure CycleCloud platform and software configuration to me to finalize, 
configure, and manage.

## Key decisions

I chose Azure CycleCloud instead of recreating the AWS ParallelCluster
environment. CycleCloud met the orchestration and autoscaling requirements
and integrated with the institution's identity, storage, and cost-control
services. Cost modeling informed the choice, along with platform fit and
the needs of a small administration team.

I kept the compute nodes homogeneous rather than creating separate pools
for serial, high-memory, and multi-node jobs. The Slurm policy used four
overlapping partitions, per-project quotas, fair-share priority, and
enforceable core-hour budgets. Any job could run on any node, while the
scheduling policy handled small and large MPI jobs without reserving
capacity for either.

Regional pricing and storage cost more than estimated. I reduced peak
capacity by roughly 11% and tracked utilization during onboarding, keeping
nearly all of the platform's planned scientific capacity within the
approved budget.

I used Spack and Lmod modules to manage the scientific software centrally
and distribute it from shared storage to autoscaled nodes. I also prepared
reusable "golden" environments for data science and machine learning.
Researchers could use the same validated stack without managing separate
installs, and software updates did not require rebuilding the node image.

As researchers started using the platform, I evaluated their storage requests 
and adapted the allocation model to better match their needs. I introduced 
a two-tier approach, with standard storage for regular users and additional 
capacity for researchers who could justify higher requirements. I am now 
working on a third tier for projects that need even more space, allowing 
researchers to request extra storage for a monthly fee. This gives researchers 
flexibility to meet their project needs while keeping shared storage manageable 
and costs under control.

## Configuration management & recovery
To make the platform easier to recover and maintain, I maintain a private Git
repository containing the cluster's key configuration files, including Slurm, 
cloud-init, Spack, environment modules, and deployment scripts. This provides a
version-controlled record of configuration changes, making it easier to trace 
regressions and recover from issues such as the loss of the scheduler node.
Although security restrictions currently prevent me from automating deployments
directly from the repository, I have structured the configuration and supporting
scripts with an Infrastructure-as-Code approach in mind, laying the groundwork 
for a more automated and reproducible deployment workflow when permissions allow.

## Documentation & operations
I also built a documentation workflow for both researchers and administrators. 
User-facing guides are [published on GitHub Pages](https://researchcomputingteam.github.io/rccg-public-docs/acc-cluster/),
with privacy checks before publication to avoid exposing internal infrastructure 
details. For administrators, I maintain a separate, private runbook written in 
Markdown and released as a versioned PDF, using Pandoc and a Git-based workflow. 
This keeps operational procedures under version control and makes updates easier
to review, reproduce, and distribute.

## Outcome

The platform is deployed and validated. CycleCloud-managed Slurm,
autoscaling compute, persistent home storage, parallel scratch,
institutional identity and MFA, accounting, and monitoring are in
production. Multi-node MPI over InfiniBand measured roughly 23,584 MB/s
at a 4 MiB message size and approximately 1.7 μs latency for small
messages. Researchers have been onboarded and are using the cluster.
